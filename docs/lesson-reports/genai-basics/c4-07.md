## 4. Truth and fidelity log

| Claim or value | Verified fact or invented? | Source (URL, doc) | Where it is disclosed in the lesson |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| BFS checks squares in order of fewest steps from Start and finds a fewest-step route on equal-cost grids | Verified fact | Russell & Norvig, *Artificial Intelligence: A Modern Approach*; UC Berkeley CS188 Search notes | `#search-strategies`, `#lab` |
| DFS follows a branch until no unchecked legal moves remain, then backtracks, and is not guaranteed to find a shortest route | Verified fact | Russell & Norvig; UC Berkeley CS188 Search notes | `#search-strategies`, `#lab` |
| Greedy Best-First Search picks from all discovered-but-unchecked squares by estimated remaining distance only, and can find a longer route than BFS | Verified fact | Russell & Norvig; UC Berkeley CS188 Informed Search notes | `#search-strategies`, `#lab`, `#limits` |
| On an equal-cost 4-direction grid, A\* combining steps-so-far with Manhattan distance finds a shortest route | Verified fact | Russell & Norvig (Manhattan distance is consistent on unweighted 4-neighbor grids) | `#limits` |
| Real AI systems can combine learned models with search (e.g., traffic-weighted routing, AlphaGo) | Verified fact | Silver et al., *Nature* 2016 (AlphaGo) | `#planning-vs-learning`, `#limits` |
| Rover robot, 5×5 `Warehouse aisle` & `Detour trap` grids, Right/Down/Left/Up neighbor order, Start+Goal included in checked count, FIFO tie-breaking for Greedy, and resulting counts (Warehouse: BFS 14/5, DFS 12/11, Greedy 8/5; Detour trap: BFS 17/6, Greedy 10/8, edited Row 4 Col 1 Greedy 7/6) | Invented illustrative toy rules (reproduced deterministically in `MazeLab.tsx` and verified in Playwright) | Disclosed in-lesson | `#what-is-search`, `#search-strategies`, `#lab` |
