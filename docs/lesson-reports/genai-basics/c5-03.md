- **Interaction:** `CalibrationLab` offers two modes plus reset:
  1. *Compare weather apps*: switch between three invented Riverside weather apps (Well calibrated, Overconfident, Underconfident) and four 100-day forecast groups (`20%`, `50%`, `70%`, `90%`) to inspect a side-by-side **Reliability bar** (Predicted % vs Observed %), the percentage-point gap, and a plain-language interpretation.
  2. *Judge the forecast*: classify three everyday invented scenarios (Parcel tracker `80%` vs `79%`, Loan-approval tool `90%` vs `65%`, Crop-frost alert `60%` vs `85%`) with specific immediate feedback in a polite live region linked via `aria-describedby`.
- **Knowledge check:**
  - Question: An invented parcel-delivery model predicts a 90% chance of same-day delivery for 100 orders; 62 arrive the same day (62%) and 38 arrive late. What does this tell you about the model's 90% predictions?
  - Correct option position: index `2` (third option: overconfident, because it predicted a 90% chance when same-day delivery actually happened only 62% of the time).
  - Wrong-option feedback:
    - Option 0 (well calibrated because >50%): explains that calibration means matching 90%, not merely exceeding 50%.
    - Option 1 (underconfident because 62% < 90%): clarifies that underconfident means predicting closer to 50% than reality.
    - Option 3 (broken because 90% means all 100 should arrive): directly corrects the "90% means certain" misconception—even a well-calibrated 90% forecast expects ~10 misses in 100.

## 5. Decisions and deviations

- **Self-contained vocabulary (`needs: []`):** Defined all five core concepts in an opening roadmap before the first image, interaction, or quiz, and replaced jargon like "sharpness", "classifiers", and "held-out data" with plain beginner wording.
- **Bidirectional overconfidence/underconfidence:** Included the `20%` forecast bucket alongside `50%`, `70%`, and `90%` so learners see that overconfidence means pushing too far from 50% in *either* direction (e.g., 20% predicted vs 35% observed), not only predicting too high.
