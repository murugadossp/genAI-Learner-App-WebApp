## 3. What was built

- **Files added:** `src/lessons/genai-basics/c2-05/{meta.json,Lesson.tsx,FitLab.tsx,fit.ts,lesson.module.css}`, `tests/web/c2-05.spec.ts`, this report.
- **Interaction:** a slider sets the number of adjustable numbers (1 to 12) of a toy curve fitted to invented noisy dots; radio buttons choose 12 or 40 examples. The learner sees the curve, training error, held-out error, the learned numbers, and held-out error at every size. With 12 examples, held-out error is lowest at 4 numbers and rises sharply at 10 to 12 while training error falls to 0. With 40 examples, large sizes are nearly harmless but still not better than a moderate size. Reset restores size 3 and 12 examples.
- **Knowledge check:** correct option is position 3 of 3 (index 2). Wrong 1: parameter count is capacity, not quality, and a bigger model need not be broken. Wrong 2: parameters are learned numbers, not stored facts.

## 5. Decisions and deviations

- A polynomial (Chebyshev basis) fit was chosen over a fake neural net because its capacity-versus-data behaviour is real and observable, and it is labelled as a simplification. Prototyped numerically before building to confirm a U-shaped held-out error with 12 examples.
- Verdict text uses no numbers so the single `role="status"` region changes only when the category changes; numbers live outside it.
- The held-out bars are capped at 0.70 and the lesson says so.

- Independent Codex review (read-only, `review` then `adversarial-review`, rounds 1-2): round 1 found nothing; round 2 raised two medium findings, both fixed in text: (a) the held-out set both picks the best size and is called the honest signal, so the lesson and lab disclosure now say it is slightly optimistic and a separate test set is needed for a final estimate; (b) "bigger means more computation per token" is now qualified for dense models, since some designs use only part of their parameters per token.
